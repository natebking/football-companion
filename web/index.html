How it works
  1. Choose the game you’re watching. Search for either team in the game list. Use Games to switch at any time.
  2. Match the timing. Open Sync TV. We start with a 45-second delay; your broadcast may need more or less.
  3. Know what to watch. Watch next highlights a player, pattern or decision that matters now. Last play explains what changed. Tap an underlined term whenever you need it.

Read the game is the default. Choose Beginner in Settings for help with the rules. The app reads play reports, not your TV picture.

No game on? Open Games → Finished games, choose a date and search for a team. Replays use the current analysis and final reports; details and timing may differ from what was available live. Availability depends on ESPN’s play reports.

More about the features

Past games compares the offense and defense using the preceding season, where enough similar plays are available. The counts match down, distance, field position, score and clock bands. Different opponents, players and coaches mean they are context, not a forecast. The separate run/pass percentage uses down, distance and field position only. The app does not watch your TV picture or know how players are lined up.

Last play explains what happened and what changed. Open Game context for the drive story, player involvement and historical comparisons. Updates can arrive late or be corrected.

Use See the idea for a diagram and a question about what you noticed. Those answers are your observations, not a graded quiz. Diagrams explain the concept; they do not reconstruct the players’ actual paths.

Explore past plays, at the bottom of the page, uses named examples from past games, including NFL charting from FTN Data via nflverse. Historical examples are separate from the live game. Passing detail is shown only when its numbers agree with the play report.

If the app gets ahead of your TV, use TV delay to hold cards, plays, and scores by 0–90 seconds. Adjust it as you watch. Prediction questions are optional in Settings; they do not measure your understanding of football.

Match TV under the field lets you choose which way the offense is attacking. The diagram keeps each team’s end consistent through possession changes in regulation and switches ends for the second and fourth quarters. Match it again after halftime or when a new overtime period starts. Use Flip field to change the view at any time.

Game journal keeps the guidance you saw and the reports that arrived, including later corrections. Open a finished game for a review. Your journal stays in this browser on this website; use Download to keep a copy. It does not follow you between the old Vercel address and fluentin.football.

Was this useful? saves feedback with the card in your journal. It stays in this browser and is included when you download the journal.

Explore plays pairs worked examples with a different past play to try. Questions use recorded facts, not video. Your first answer in each attempt is saved separately in Practice history, where you can download or clear it. These answers do not change the live analysis.
