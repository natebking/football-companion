How it works

Pick a game, then keep watching on TV. Read the game connects the situation with patterns in the plays reported so far. It is the default; Beginner explains the rules. Tap an underlined term for a definition in either mode.

Past games compares the offense and defense using the preceding season, where enough similar plays are available. The counts match down, distance, field position, score and clock bands. Different opponents, players and coaches mean they are context, not a forecast. The separate run/pass percentage uses down, distance and field position only. The app does not watch your TV picture or know how players are lined up.

Recent plays explains what happened and what changed. The drive story connects those plays, including progress from penalties. Open So far in this game to see which players and parts of the field have been involved. Updates can arrive late or be corrected.

Use See the idea for a diagram and a question about what you noticed. Those answers are your observations, not a graded quiz. Diagrams explain the concept; they do not reconstruct the players’ actual paths.

Explore past plays, at the bottom of the page, uses named examples from past games, including NFL charting from FTN Data via nflverse. Historical examples are separate from the live game. Passing detail is shown only when its numbers agree with the play report.

If the app gets ahead of your TV, use TV delay to hold cards, plays, and scores by 0–90 seconds. Adjust it as you watch. Prediction questions are optional in Games; they do not measure your understanding of football.

Game journal keeps the guidance you saw and the reports that arrived, including later corrections. Open a finished game for a review. Your journal stays in this browser on this website; use Download to keep a copy. It does not follow you between the old Vercel address and fluentin.football.

Was this useful? saves feedback with the card in your journal. It stays in this browser and is included when you download the journal.
